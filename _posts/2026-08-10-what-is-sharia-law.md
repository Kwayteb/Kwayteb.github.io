Sharia refers to the body of religious law derived from the Quran and the Hadith (recorded teachings and practices of the Prophet Muhammad). It covers far more than criminal or civil law in the Western sense — it also addresses worship, diet, family life, and personal conduct.

Sharia is not applied uniformly. Different schools of Islamic jurisprudence (madhhabs) — such as Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali in Sunni Islam, or Ja'fari in Shia Islam — interpret source texts differently, and the degree to which Sharia is codified into a country's formal legal system varies widely from one nation to another.
