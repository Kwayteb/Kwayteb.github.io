Formal state law is law enacted and enforced by a government's official institutions — legislatures, courts, and executive agencies. It typically includes a written constitution, statutes passed by a legislature, regulations issued by agencies, and case law built up through court rulings.

Unlike religious law, formal state law derives its authority from the state itself rather than a religious text, and it generally applies to everyone within the state's jurisdiction regardless of personal belief. Many countries operate a mixed system, where formal state law coexists with religious or customary law in specific areas such as family or inheritance matters.
